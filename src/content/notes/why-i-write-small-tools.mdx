Small tools are a way of respecting context. They do one useful thing, fit into existing workflows, and leave room for the next person to compose them differently.

## Fewer opinions

A small tool should have strong defaults and weak ownership over the rest of the workflow.

## Better failure

When a tool is narrow, its failure modes are easier to see. That makes it easier to trust.

## The compound effect

The best tools become invisible infrastructure for thought. They remove one bit of friction every day.
